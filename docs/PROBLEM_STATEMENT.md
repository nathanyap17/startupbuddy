## One line
**Young Malaysians who want to start a business get stuck between "I have an idea" and "I'm talking to the right people about a validated plan", because nobody checks the idea against their real neighbourhood and nobody helps them take the first concrete step.**

## What is happening now (status quo)
Take Aisyah, a final-year student in Kuching who says "I want to sell kolo mee near Padungan".
1. **The idea stays vague.** She doesn't know who exactly would buy, at what price, or why from her rather than the stalls already there.
2. **She checks the market by gut feel.** Without asking, she can't see how many kolo mee stalls are within walking distance, how well they're rated, or who lives and works around there.
3. **Generic AI tools give generic answers.** ChatGPT or a plan generator writes a tidy business plan that would read the same for Kuala Lumpur or Jakarta. It knows nothing about her street, the council licence or the local programmes.
4. **Help exists but is scattered.** Licensing (SSM, local council, food handler course), funding (TEKUN, SME Corp, state bodies) and mentoring (MYStartup and others) sit on separate websites. She doesn't know which one applies to her, or where to start.
5. **Nobody challenges the plan.** She has no mentor, investor or experienced owner to say "your price won't cover your rent".
6. **She never takes the first step.** No meeting is booked and no email is sent, so the idea quietly dies, or she launches unprepared and joins the businesses that close early.

**What changes with StartupBuddy:** in one session she gets a problem statement grounded in her actual area, a full business model, a tough review from five perspectives, and her first meetings and emails prepared (sent only after she approves).

## Statement

**Aspiring founders in Malaysia, from students and fresh graduates to would-be hawkers in Kuching, rarely get from a vague idea to a validated, locally grounded business with a first real conversation booked.** They do not lack app builders or plan templates. What they lack is (1) validation grounded in *their* neighbourhood (who buys, who already competes on the same street, which licences and programmes apply) and (2) a concrete path to the right local people. The help that exists hands them information; nobody does the work with them.

**Measurable value:** time from intent to (a) a confirmed problem statement, (b) a reviewed business model and (c) the first booked stakeholder meeting, in one session instead of weeks.

## Non-obvious insight
Generic AI validators answer "is this a good idea in general?". A kolo mee stall succeeds or fails on **the 500 m around it**: competitor density and ratings, foot traffic, local licensing (council premises licence, food handler training, SSM registration) and which local programme (e.g. TEKUN, SME Corp, Sarawak bodies) will actually take a meeting. Grounding plus action is the gap.

## Who exactly
Primary demo persona: a final-year student in Kuching who wants to start a food business or a small tech startup, with no network and no prior business training.
